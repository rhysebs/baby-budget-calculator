Assumptions worth knowing
  • Jul 2026 ($72k), Aug 2026 ($77k), and Sep 2026 ($85k) are actual recorded balances, not modelled. The model projects forward from the September figure.
  • Living costs aren't a fixed number — they're whatever's left of current income after the current mortgage and your chosen savings rate. Raising the savings slider lowers living costs by the same amount.
  • While both incomes are coming in, the savings rate holds flat even after the move, so the larger mortgage is absorbed by trimming living costs rather than by saving less.
  • Once partner leave starts, savings contributions stop entirely. That money goes to daily spending, and any shortfall draws straight from the balance.
  • The car purchase and all moving/setup costs land in the same month as the house move, as one lump sum.
  • Partner returns to work in March 2028 regardless of when leave starts, so an earlier leave start makes the unpaid stretch longer, not the paid one.
  • Paid parental leave runs for 6 months from the leave start date, then unpaid until the return date.
  • The mortgage rate and term are fixed for the whole period modelled — no refinancing or rate changes assumed.
  • The deposit on the new house is always 20% of its price, so the new loan is always 80%. The sale price does not change the loan; it changes how much cash is left after the deposit is paid.
  • Sale proceeds are the sale price, minus agent commission, minus fixed selling costs, minus the $400,000 still owed on the current house. That net amount goes into the cash balance, and the 20% deposit comes straight back out of it.
  • If the sale nets less than the deposit, the gap is covered from savings. The sale slider turns red until the "savings put toward deposit" slider covers that gap.
  • Baby's year-1 costs are spread evenly across the 12 months from birth, not front-loaded for big early purchases.
  • The savings balance earns no interest or investment return in this model.
  • Sale and purchase settle in the same month, with no bridging period or bridging finance cost.
  • Mid-month dates are handled proportionally — a leave start on the 15th counts roughly half that month at each income level.
